2. Problem

People want AI, but:

  1. Data can't leave the country. Banks, clinics and offices can't send personal data to foreign clouds. Uzbekistan's personal-data law (amended by Law ZRU-666, in force 15 April 2021) requires citizens' data to be kept on servers inside the country.[3] Nearly 60% of AI leaders cite cross-border data rules as a major challenge (NTT DATA, May 2026).[4]
  2. Cloud AI is expensive. $20–30 per person every month: ChatGPT Business[5] and Claude Team[6] cost $20 billed yearly, and Microsoft 365 Copilot costs $18–30 on top of a Microsoft 365 licence.[7]
  3. Local AI is for experts. Free tools exist, but people must know which model their computer can run and how to set it up.

So people risk their data, overpay, or go without AI.

3. Product

One app. Private AI on the computer you have. Runs on any mid-range computer: it checks the machine and picks the model that runs well on it. Private and offline: nothing you type leaves the computer, and no account is needed. From laptop to company server: free for people; companies run it on their own server.

Appendix A. Hardware, speed and capacity

We don't limit people to a few models. localSpace runs open models from many makers (Qwen, Gemma, Mistral, Llama, Phi, gpt-oss and more), from 1B to 122B parameters. Bring your own model (0.2.1) loads any open model in GGUF format from Hugging Face. The app checks the computer and picks the best model that fits.

A.1 Model classes

A mixture-of-experts (MoE) model uses only a few of its parts for each word, so it reads less data per word and runs faster than a dense model of the same size. File sizes: Qwen3.5[23, 24, 25, 26], Gemma 4[27], gpt-oss[28, 29], Qwen3.5 122B-A10B[30].

How we got this

Speed for one person = memory bandwidth ÷ data read per word × efficiency. Example: 448 GB/s ÷ 6.2 GB × 0.5 = 36 tokens a second, about 27 words a second. The efficiency 0.38 is measured on our two laptops; 0.5 for desktop cards with dense models and 0.3 for mixture-of-experts models, both from a public RTX 3090 benchmark.[40] Chats at once = the smaller of two limits; team size = 3–5 × chats at once. Appendix C. Method and glossary

C.1 Market: TAM, SAM, SOM

  1. TAM 2025 = $8.65B. This is what companies worldwide spent on language-model AI in 2025 (GMI Insights).[1] We use it because local AI competes for the same budget: a company either pays a cloud service or runs AI itself.
  2. TAM after 2025 = $8.65B × (1 + growth)years after 2025. Each case uses a growth rate from a published report: min, 13.5% a year (GMI Insights)[1]; mid, 20.2% a year (Market.us, in-country "sovereign" AI)[11]; max, 28.3% a year (Research and Markets).[12] Example, mid 2030: $8.65B × 1.2025 = $21.70B. 2025 is the same in every case because it has already happened; only the future growth differs.
  3. SAM = TAM × 0.495%. There's no published figure for AI spending in Central Asia, so we use its share of the world economy: $570.6B out of $115.3T (IMF, 2025).[2, 10] Kazakhstan $306.0B, Uzbekistan $147.1B, Turkmenistan $77.4B, Kyrgyzstan $22.6B and Tajikistan $17.5B. We keep that share flat. It could be lower, because companies here adopt AI more slowly than in the US or Europe. It could also be higher, because these economies grow faster and personal data must stay in the country, which favours local AI. The min case covers the lower side. Example, 2025: $8.65B × 0.495% = $42.8M.
  4. SOM = paid seats × $108 a year ($9 a month × 12). The seats come from counting real organisations in Uzbekistan.[13, 14, 15, 44] Example, mid 2029: 3 banks × 300 + 10 universities × 200 + 50 clinics × 20 + 100 companies × 20 = 5,900 seats; 5,900 × $108 = $637,200. The min plan has about one sixth of the mid seats, for slow sales. The max plan has about twice the mid seats, for faster sales. In 2025 and 2026 we have no revenue, so the SOM is $0.

C.2 Hardware: speed, people served, cost per person

Worked example: the default office desktop with an RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB and a 9B model.

  1. Price: the middle of the October 2026 range, ($1,700 + $2,000) ÷ 2 = $1,850.[16, 22]
  2. Speed for one person = memory speed ÷ model size × efficiency. Every word the AI writes reads the whole model from memory once, so memory speed sets the pace. Calculation: 448 GB/s ÷ 6.2 GB × 0.5 = 36 tokens a second, about 27 words a second. The efficiency (0.5) comes from a public RTX 3090 benchmark.[40] On laptops we use 0.38, which we measured ourselves.
  3. Chats at once = the smaller of two limits. Memory: (16 GB − 6.2 GB model − 1.5 GB kept free) ÷ 0.5 GB per conversation = 16. Speed: when people share the machine, it produces about 2.5× more in total. 36 × 2.5 ÷ 15 tokens a second each (a comfortable reading speed) = 6. So: 6 chats at once.
  4. Team size = 3–5 × chats at once = 18–30 people. In an office, only about 20–33% of people are waiting for an answer at the same moment; the rest are reading, typing or doing other work.
  5. Hardware cost per person = $1,850 ÷ 24 (the middle of 18–30) = $77 once. Spread over 3 years of use, that's $77 ÷ 36 = $2.14 a month. This money goes to a shop, once. It isn't localSpace's price.

Models partly in normal memory are much slower; our 9B on the 4 GB laptop ran at 9 tokens a second. Only cells marked "measured" are measured. Everything else is an estimate we'll confirm with our own test kit.

Example: a team of 24 people. Software, per year: cloud AI $20 × 24 × 12 = $5,760; localSpace $9 × 24 × 12 = $2,592. Hardware, per year: cloud AI none; localSpace $1,850 ÷ 3 years = $617. Total per year: $5,760 against $3,209. Over 3 years: $17,280 against $9,626. The company saves about $2,550 a year (44%) and its data never leaves the building.

C.6 Words and tokens

  • AI models count text in tokens. One token is about three quarters of an English word, so 25 tokens a second is about 19 words a second.
  • Uzbek and Russian words use more tokens than English words, so in those languages the words-a-second figure is a little lower.

C.8 Glossary

TAM
Total addressable market: the global market. Here, company spending on language-model AI worldwide.
SAM
Serviceable available market: the part we can reach. Here, Central Asia.
SOM
Serviceable obtainable market: what we plan to win. Here, paid seats in Uzbekistan.
Token
The unit AI models count text in; about three quarters of an English word.
Mixture-of-experts (MoE)
A model that uses only a few of its parts for each word, so it reads less data per word and runs faster than a dense model of the same size.
Dense model
A model that uses all of its parts for every word.
GGUF
The file format open models use with the llama.cpp engine, which localSpace runs on.
H100
An NVIDIA data-centre graphics card, rented by the hour for training.
Fine-tuning
Further training of an existing open model on new examples, such as Uzbek text or tool-use examples.
Seat
One person at a paying company, at $9 a month.
