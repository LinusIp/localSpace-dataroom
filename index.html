Problem

People want AI, but:

  1. Data can't leave the country.

    Banks, clinics and offices can't send personal data to foreign clouds. Uzbekistan's personal-data law (amended by Law ZRU-666, in force 15 April 2021) requires citizens' data to be kept on servers inside the country.

    Nearly 60% of AI leaders cite cross-border data rules as a major challenge (NTT DATA, May 2026).

  2. Cloud AI is expensive.

    $20–30 per person every month: ChatGPT Business and Claude Team cost $20 billed yearly, and Microsoft 365 Copilot costs $18–30 on top of a Microsoft 365 licence.

  3. Local AI is for experts.

    Free tools exist, but people must know which model their computer can run and how to set it up.

So people risk their data, overpay, or go without AI.

Solution

One app. Private AI on the computer you have.

  • Runs on any mid-range computer.

    It checks the machine and picks the model that runs well on it.

  • Private and offline.

    Nothing you type leaves the computer. No account needed.

  • From laptop to company server.

    Free for people; companies run it on their own server.

Works today In the public preview

  • chat with open models from several makers (Qwen3.5, Gemma 4, Ministral), from 0.8B to 9B, chosen to fit the computer;
  • automatic model choice for the computer;
  • attaching pictures and screenshots;
  • English, Russian and Uzbek;
  • any graphics card (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel, through Vulkan) or none;
  • chats kept when updating.

Recommendations

  • For people

    Any laptop runs small models; a gaming laptop with an 8 GB card runs the 7–9B class well.

  • Small company default

    Office desktop with an RTX 5060 Ti 16 GB, about $1,700–2,000, for a team of 18–30.

  • Best value

    A used RTX 3090 24 GB desktop, about $2,300–2,600, for a team of 36–60.

  • Biggest models on a budget

    The 128 GB AMD mini PC runs 100–122B models for a small team.

  • Not recommended now

    The RTX 5090, about $9,344 new in October 2026 after memory shortages.

How we calculated it
  • Speed for one person: memory bandwidth ÷ data read per word × efficiency.
  • Efficiencies:
    • 0.38 is measured on our two laptops;
    • 0.5 for desktop cards with dense models and 0.3 for mixture-of-experts models, both from a public RTX 3090 benchmark.
  • Models partly in normal memory: much slower; our 9B on the 4 GB laptop ran at 9 tokens a second.
  • Chats at once: the smaller of two limits:
    • conversations that fit in the memory left over;
    • shared speed (2.5× one person) ÷ 15 tokens a second each.
  • Team size: 3–5 × chats at once.
  • Measured vs estimated: only cells marked "measured" are measured. Everything else is an estimate we'll confirm with our own test kit.

Business

$9 a person a month, billed yearly

  • One company server for everyone, so employees don't need strong laptops.
  • Company sign-in and admin.
  • Shared company documents with access rules.
  • Set up and supported by us.
  • Every company starts with a free 30-day trial.

Free runs on one computer. Business gives the whole company one AI server.

Why $9

  • It's less than half of cloud AI ($20 a person a month billed yearly).
  • We don't pay for every message: the AI runs on the customer's own hardware. Our costs are building, updating and supporting the software, not running models.
  • Hardware is the customer's own, or bought once. Section 6 shows a team of about 18–30 served by a ~$2,000 desktop.

GPU plan · $6,000

The GPU plan: lowering the hardware bar

The smaller and smarter the model, the cheaper the computer that runs it. Every step below aims to give people and companies the same quality on cheaper hardware.

These are goals, not results. Each one is measured with our own test kit before we claim it.

Fine-tuning steps and H100 hours
StepWorkWhat it meansH100 hours
1Make training examples with a big open modelA large open model (gpt-oss-120b / Qwen3.5 122B) writes Uzbek and Russian answers and agent tool-use examples; native speakers spot-check them45
2Teach small models more UzbekContinued training of 2B, 4B and 9B models on about 2 billion tokens each: public Uzbek web text (FineWeb-2: 2.3 million Uzbek documents, Latin and Cyrillic) mixed with Russian and English85
3Teach them chat and agent tool use (distillation)Small models learn from the big model's examples in English, Russian and Uzbek: following instructions, calling tools reliably, reading files; 6 rounds for 2B, 4B and 9B153
4Make models smaller: low-bit trainingQuantization-aware training to about 2 bits per weight (ternary), so a 9B model needs about 2.5-3 GB instead of 6.2 GB: 10B tokens for the 4B, 5B tokens for the 9B339
5Tune one strong model for company serversUzbek and agent tuning of a fast 30-35B mixture-of-experts model (about 8,000 tokens/s per GPU), 3 rounds of 600M tokens62
6Test every versionConvert to GGUF, run our own test kit for speed and mistakes in three languages, test on big GPUs100
Repeats: failed runs and tuning (about +50%)392

Cost

  • 1,176 hours × $3–5 an hour = about $3,528–5,880. The $6,000 budget assumes the upper end, because GPU prices rose in 2026.
  • H100 prices in October 2026: $1.99–3.99 on RunPod and $3.29–4.29 on Lambda (comparison).
  • If idrock gives us GPU cluster time, this line shrinks and the money moves to pilots.

Three goals each measured with our own test kit before we claim it

  • Good Uzbek on small machines

    TargetA tuned 4B model answers Uzbek as well as today's 9B on our test set

    What it changesGood Uzbek at ~19 words/s on 4 GB laptop cards (today's 9B: ~7) and ~5 words/s with no graphics card

  • A 9B model in 3 GB

    TargetLow-bit 9B keeps quality within a few points of the normal 9B on our test set

    What it changesFits fully on 4 GB cards; about twice the speed on any machine; a company server holds about twice as many conversations

  • Agents on mid-range computers

    TargetSmall models call tools correctly in 9 of 10 tries on our agent test set

    What it changesThe localSpace agent works on ordinary laptops, not only on big GPUs

Why we think it works

  • A fine-tuned 770M model beat the 540B PaLM (few-shot) on a benchmark: over 700x smaller (Distilling Step-by-Step, 2023).
  • BitNet b1.58 2B4T: 0.4 GB of memory vs 1.4-4.8 GB for similar full-precision models, with similar benchmark scores (2025).

What we'll use

  • open models whose licences allow fine-tuning (for example Qwen, Apache 2.0);
  • public Uzbek text (FineWeb-2: 1,098,843 Latin-script and 1,247,285 Cyrillic-script Uzbek documents);
  • examples written by a large open model and spot-checked by native speakers.
